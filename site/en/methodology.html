How we calculate the share of non-EU components

How we apply the 35 % rule for SAFE, EDIP and related instruments. We work from the text of the regulations and from the method the Commission has published for EDIP. Where the rules are silent, we say so openly and describe what we do.

01The rule in the legal texts

The cost of components originating outside the Union, EEA EFTA States and Ukraine shall not be higher than 35 % of the estimated cost of the components of the end product.

Council Regulation (EU) 2025/1106 (SAFE), Article 16(10) [1]

SAFE also requires that no component comes from a third country that is contrary to the security and defence interests of the EU. This is a separate condition, independent of the 35 %.

SAFE recital 19 states that the Commission “could establish guidance” on the calculation. As of 18 October 2026, there is no public method for SAFE.

The same rule in other instruments

  • EDIP, Regulation (EU) 2025/2643, Article 10(3) and (4): components originating “outside the Union and associated countries”, same 35 % threshold. Recital 27: raw materials are not components. [2]
  • EDIP – Ukraine Support Instrument (USI), Article 27: “outside the Union and Ukraine”. [2]
  • Ukraine loan, Regulation (EU) 2026/467, Article 13(4)(g): same as SAFE (EU, EEA EFTA states, Ukraine). [3]
  • EU–Canada agreement on participation in SAFE, Article 5(8), applicable from 1 August 2026: Canadian components may exceed 35 %; components from the EU, EEA EFTA states and Ukraine must make up at least 20 %; components from other third countries no more than 35 % and no more than the EU share. [4]

What is at stake

Under SAFE, suspension of payment; under EDIP, reduction or termination of the grant. False information can lead to exclusion from EU funding.

02The EDIP method step by step

The Commission has published a detailed method in call EDF-EDIP-P-2026-LS-IRA (version 1.2 of 10 June 2026, pages 9–10) and in the training slides “Origin of Components” (5/2026) [5] [6]. We use it for EDIP and, until there is SAFE guidance, for SAFE as well.

1. What a component is

Substances, materials (excluding raw materials), parts, assemblies or software incorporated into the product.

2. Which parts count

Only parts purchased directly by the manufacturer, i.e. from first-tier (tier-1) suppliers, regardless of their importance. Parts your suppliers buy are not counted separately.

3. How origin is determined

  • Imported part: from customs documents or purchase contracts.
  • Part bought in the EU (including via a trader or within a group): the country of the last substantial, economically justified transformation under the Union Customs Code, i.e. non-preferential origin under Article 60 of Regulation (EU) No 952/2013 [7]. The supplier holds this information, so the supplier evidences it with a declaration.

4. Which cost

Cost from invoices, contracts or customs documents, excluding transport, duties and taxes. Line cost is quantity per end product × unit price × exchange rate to the project currency.

5. Two methods

Method (a) cost of non-EU tier-1 partscost of all tier-1 parts≤ 35 %
Method (b) cost of non-EU tier-1 partscost of the components of the end product≤ 35 %

The “cost of the components of the end product” is the cost of all tier-1 parts plus the total manufacturing cost of sub-assemblies made in-house, excluding final assembly. In the calculation these are lines of type inhouse: labour and overhead for sub-assemblies, without purchased parts already listed as tier-1. If there are no such lines, method (b) is not used.

Meeting one of the methods is enough. But the condition must hold throughout: “The condition must be complied with during the entire duration of the action.”

03The Commission's example

The Commission's slides show the same product calculated both ways: 92 / 214 = 43 % (method (a) fails) and 92 / 275 = 34 % (method (b) passes). The exact figures are 42.99 % and 33.45 %. The difference between the denominators, 275 − 214 = 61, is the manufacturing cost of in-house sub-assemblies.

05Evidence status and scenarios

Every line has one of three statuses. These combine into three scenarios, so you see the result as a range rather than a single number.

Documented
The country of origin is filled in, the evidence is a customs document, a supplier declaration or a contract, and if the declaration has a validity date, it has not expired.
Stated, not documented
The country of origin is filled in, but the evidence is missing, is only an assumption, or the declaration has expired.
Unknown
The country of origin is missing.
Worstaudit
Only “documented” lines with a home origin count as home. Everything else, stated, undocumented or unknown, counts as non-EU. This is the result when only documented evidence is accepted.
Expected
Uses the stated origin; if missing, the expected origin; if that is missing too, the supplier's country. Lines with no information at all count as non-EU.
Best
Uses the stated origin; lines of unknown origin count as home. This shows the ceiling: with these parts it cannot turn out better.

For each scenario and each method we calculate the numerator (cost outside home countries), the denominator, the share and whether it passes. This gives the verdict:

  • Compliant the worst (audit) case passes under at least one method.
  • Depends on evidence the expected or best case passes, the worst does not. Evidence will decide.
  • Not compliant not even the best case passes. Evidence will not help; some non-EU parts need to be replaced.

Headroom tells you how much non-EU cost could still be added: 0.35 × denominator − numerator, for the more favourable method in the worst and the expected case. Negative headroom is the amount that is missing.

What “documented” does not mean

Documented means that a valid piece of evidence of origin exists for the line. It is not a legal confirmation of origin. The supplier who signed a declaration is responsible for its content; your company is responsible for what it submits to the contracting authority or the Commission.

06Minimum evidence set

You do not need to chase every supplier. We look for the smallest number of lines whose documentation is enough for the worst (audit) case to pass as well.

  1. We calculate the set for both methods and use the one that comes out smaller.
  2. Select the undocumented lines (stated and unknown) whose expected origin is home. Only for these is it worth chasing evidence.
  3. Sort them by cost, largest first, and add them until: numerator − documented cost ≤ 0.35 × denominator. Largest first gives the smallest possible number of lines.
  4. The result is a list of lines, their count and the cost covered. We group the lines by supplier and flag suppliers in the minimum set as priority.

If even all such lines are not enough, evidence alone will not help. We then calculate the substitution: the smallest number of non-EU lines (under the expected case, largest cost first) that would need to be replaced with a home source for the expected case to pass.

08Open questions and how we handle them

Some points are not expressly settled by the regulations or the published method, or have to be assessed for the specific product. Here is what we do. Every choice is recorded in the description of the approach, so it can be traced.

SAFE has no official method yet

We use the EDIP approach because none has been published for SAFE and the wording of the rule is the same. We calculate both methods and state which one passes. Once the Commission issues SAFE guidance, we recalculate accordingly.

Transport and duty in in-house manufacturing cost

Tier-1 cost excludes transport, duties and taxes. The total manufacturing cost (TMC) of in-house sub-assemblies, however, includes inbound transport and non-recoverable duty according to the Commission. The method does not explain this inconsistency. We therefore count tier-1 parts without transport and duty, take the manufacturing cost of sub-assemblies as the Commission defines it, and show transport and duty within it separately. If leaving them out would change the verdict, we show the result both ways.

Where raw materials end and materials begin

Raw materials are not components (EDIP recital 27); materials are. Where exactly the line runs, for example for semi-finished products, has to be assessed item by item. We flag and justify every item excluded as a raw material. For borderline items we calculate both variants and use the less favourable one in the conclusion until there is support for the more favourable one.

Contracts covering several product variants

The rule refers to the end product. When a contract covers several variants or configurations, we calculate each separately and point out the one that comes out worst. We do not add up or average variants unless the contracting authority confirms that approach.

Compliance for the whole duration

Supplier declarations in our template are valid for at most 24 months and include a commitment to report changes. Expired declarations count as undocumented in the calculation; declarations expiring within 90 days are flagged in advance.

Exchange rates

We agree the exchange rates with you in advance and record them in the description of the approach.

09What the output is and is not

  • The output is a calculation based on documents and supplier declarations, plus a description of the approach. {{BRAND}} does not give legal opinions.
  • Disputed origin determinations are reviewed with a customs specialist.
  • A declaration of non-preferential origin based on our template is not a preferential declaration under Implementing Regulation (EU) 2015/2447.
  • You can repeat the calculation at any time in the calculator. It applies the rules described here.
