01Why now

The rule applies to money that is already flowing

Both SAFE and EDIP make funding conditional on where components come from. If you cannot prove compliance, the payment is at risk.

The cost of components originating outside the Union, EEA EFTA States and Ukraine shall not be higher than 35 % of the estimated cost of the components of the end product.

Council Regulation (EU) 2025/1106 (SAFE), Article 16(10) · EUR-Lex

EDIP (Regulation (EU) 2025/2643, Article 10(3) and (4)) sets the same rule for components originating outside the Union and associated countries. SAFE also requires that no component comes from a third country that is contrary to the security and defence interests of the EU.

Limited information on underlying costs can also create practical challenges for verifying and confirming compliance with certain EU preference clauses.

European Court of Auditors, Review 01/2026, paragraph 71 · PDF

What is at stake: under SAFE, suspension of payment; under EDIP, reduction or termination of the grant. False information can lead to exclusion from EU funding.

06Methodology

We follow the Commission's method and document every step

  • A component is any substance, material, part, assembly or software incorporated into the product. Raw materials do not count.
  • Tier-1 only: parts bought directly by the manufacturer count, regardless of their importance.
  • Origin of an imported part comes from customs documents or purchase contracts. For a part bought in the EU, the country of the last substantial transformation decides (non-preferential origin, Article 60 of the Union Customs Code).
  • Cost comes from invoices, contracts or customs documents, excluding transport, duties and taxes.
  • SAFE has no official method yet. We use the EDIP approach and document that choice.

07Questions

Frequently asked questions

Does SAFE have an official calculation method?

Not yet. SAFE recital 19 says the Commission “could establish guidance” on the calculation. As of 18 October 2026, none has been published.

The Commission has published a detailed method for EDIP. We apply it to SAFE as well: we calculate both methods, document the choice, and recalculate once SAFE guidance is published.

What counts as a component?

Under the EDIP method: substances, materials (excluding raw materials), parts, assemblies or software incorporated into the product. Raw materials are not components (EDIP recital 27).

Only parts you buy directly (tier-1) count, regardless of how important they are. Parts your suppliers buy are not counted separately; they are reflected in the origin of the part you buy from them.

What if a supplier does not respond?

In the worst (audit) case, their parts count as non-EU. But you do not need an answer from everyone. The minimum evidence set shows which lines are enough to document for the result to pass even in the worst case, so you only chase the suppliers that matter.

For imported parts, customs documents help. If even that is not enough, we calculate how many parts would need to be replaced with parts from a home country. Suppliers who are unsure how to determine origin can use our Supplier service.

Do you need our drawings or technical documentation?

No. We work only with commercial data: part number or category, supplier, price, country of origin and evidence. We do not take drawings, specifications, technical documentation, classified information or ITAR-controlled data. Part numbers can be replaced with pseudonyms.

Is this legal advice?

No. {{BRAND}} does not give legal opinions. The output is a calculation based on documents and supplier declarations, together with a description of the approach we used. Disputed origin determinations are reviewed with a customs specialist.

Who signs the origin determination?

The party that knows the origin: the supplier. They sign a declaration of non-preferential origin within the meaning of Article 60 of the Union Customs Code; in our template it is valid for at most 24 months and includes a commitment to report changes. For imported parts, origin follows from customs documents or purchase contracts.

We collect the documents, check that they are complete and valid, and calculate from them. Your company remains responsible for what it submits to the contracting authority or the Commission.

What about Switzerland, the UK, Norway, Ukraine and Canada?
  • Switzerland and the United Kingdom are not home countries under any of the rules. Their parts count towards the 35 %.
  • Norway is home under SAFE (as an EEA EFTA state) and under EDIP. Iceland and Liechtenstein are home under SAFE.
  • Ukraine is home under SAFE, the Ukraine loan and the EDIP Ukraine Support Instrument, but not under the EDIP programme itself.
  • Canada: the EU–Canada agreement on participation in SAFE (applicable from 1 August 2026) has its own rule. Canadian components may exceed 35 %; components from the EU, EEA EFTA states and Ukraine must make up at least 20 %; components from other third countries no more than 35 % and no more than the EU share.

The calculator has a separate rule pack for each of these cases. See the overview in the methodology.

Is meeting one method enough?

Under the EDIP method, yes: either method (a) or method (b) has to pass. But the condition must hold “during the entire duration of the action”, so the calculation needs to be kept up to date.

What exactly does “documented” mean?

A line is documented when it has a country of origin and evidence (a customs document, a supplier declaration or a contract) and the declaration is still valid. The worst (audit) case counts only documented lines as home and everything else as non-EU.

How long does it take?

The calculator gives you a result immediately. A Quick Check takes up to 10 working days (up to 500 lines), an Evidence Pack 4–6 weeks. Supplier responses take the longest, so we contact the suppliers the result depends on first.
